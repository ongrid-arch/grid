# 2026-11-27: one building of a site of many, read and changed on its own (CMD-47)

## For people

- On a plan of many buildings (a housing site, a resort, a campus), an agent reads and changes one building at a time and never has to read the whole plan: it reads the building back as rooms text of its own, writes it back changed while every other building stays in the file as it was, adds a storey on top of it like one of its floors (the stair and the roof going up with it), takes it out with everything on its floors, or places a copy of it on another lot. A villa swapped for three townhouses is one call (CMD-47).

## For agents

- `grid_describe` takes `building` with `as=rooms`: that building's rooms text alone (its floors, rooms, walls, openings, stairs, items, bays and notes, and its building line), its `based on` line the building's own fingerprint. `building` without `as=rooms` is refused.
- New tool `grid_write_building`: `building`, then `rooms` or `roomsFile`, and `replace`. The building's floors and every part on them are replaced by what the text writes, and the other buildings' records are kept as the file has them. Text read from another version of the building is refused; text with no `based on` line over a building with rooms needs `replace=true`; a text with no floor line, one with a floor another building holds, or one with a plot, north or base line is refused, nothing written. Building lines in the text write those buildings in its place; with none, its floors are the building named, its name and pad kept, and an id the plan does not have adds it. An id another building holds is given its floor's id in front; the answer lists each and `renamed` in the data maps them, with `building` and `buildingsWritten`.
- New tool `grid_add_storey`: `building`, `like` (a floor of it; its top floor when left out), `id`, `name`. A floor on top, at the next level and stacked on the old top, with its walls, rooms, openings, patterns, items, columns, bays and flats copied with ids of the new floor (`c1-bed` becomes `c2-bed`, `bed` becomes `c2-bed`); walls and columns through the old top rise through it, a stair reaching the old top gets a flight on to it, and the old top's roof, parapet and `above` move up. `floor` in the data.
- New tool `grid_remove_building`: `id`. The building, its floors and everything on them, and the stairs and ramps between it and another building; a flat keeps its rooms in other buildings. `grid_set_building remove` still takes out the record only.
- New tool `grid_place_building`: `from`, `id`, `move` (`dx,dy`) or `at` (`x,y`, where the south-west corner of its walls' centre lines goes), `name`, `pad`. A copy of every floor and part, each id of its own (a floor id starting with the copied building's id takes the new one's, else the new id in front), moved and never turned; `moved` and `overlaps` (buildings whose walls' box it overlaps) in the data. A copy over another building's walls is refused by the drawing check.
- The tool server lists 63 tools; the four come after `grid_set_building` and before `grid_add_item`. No new code in the record.
