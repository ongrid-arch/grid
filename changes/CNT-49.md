# 2026-11-27: the ground per side, a fall or a plot edge's levels, read at every wall (CNT-49)

## For people

- A plan on a slope can say where the ground is round the building: at its sides (`ground north 0 south -4.6`), as a fall (`ground slope 30% toward south`), or along edges of the plot, a street that falls along its edge (`ground edge 1 0 to -3`). `grid_set_site` takes the same as `ground_north` … `ground_west`, `slope` and `toward`, and `ground_edges`. Grid takes the one even slope that fits them, and says how far it is off any level it cannot meet (CNT-49).
- Every check and picture then reads the ground at the wall in question: a bedroom window dug into the hill is named as below the ground while the walk-out garden door is not; a terrace door over the falling garden is named as above the ground and is not a way out, while each door along a falling street is read against the street in front of it; a walk-out lower floor is a ground floor, in the footprint and the storey count; the height is measured from the lowest ground round the building; the facades and sections draw the ground as a sloped line and the 3D view tilts it. A plan with one ground level reads exactly as before.

## For agents

- The plan file: `site.groundSides` (`north`, `east`, `south`, `west`), `site.fall` (`slope` in percent, `toward` one of the eight compass points of the drawing) and `site.groundEdges` (`edge`, `start`, `end`), all optional and never defaulted (C7 widened, no new convention number). Sides and compass points are the drawing's, north up it, as the facades name them.
- `grid_set_site` gains `ground_north`, `ground_east`, `ground_south`, `ground_west`, `slope` with `toward`, and `ground_edges` (`"<edge> <level> [to <level>]"`); `remove` takes `groundSides`, `fall` and `groundEdges`. Its answer says the ground's range under the building and its fall, and how far the slope taken is off a stated level when they lie on no one even slope.
- `grid_check`: on a slope `opening_below_ground` reads the ground at the opening's own wall, and `door_to_outside_above_ground` is also said for a door out of a floor on the ground that stands more than 2 m over the ground at its own place, with that height in `elevation`; that door is no way out. A floor whose level lies within the ground under its walls is a ground floor.
- `grid_facade` data: `ground.fall` (`at`, the level at u = 0, and `perMetre`) on a slope, and its text says the ground's level at the face's two ends. `grid_section` data: `ground.fall` (`at`, the level at x = 0, and `perMetre` along the line), and its levels line says the ground's level at the line's two ends. `grid_measure what=access` and the brief's step line read the step in at a door against the ground at that door. No new code in the record.
