Grid (ongrid) and ArchLang

Dated 23 November 2026. Facts about ArchLang come from a research note read on 16 November 2026 and have not been re-checked since; check the other tool's own site for what it does today. This page describes tools; it does not rank them or say one is better.

What the research note says about ArchLang

  • It describes itself as “floor plans as code”.
  • On Glama (a directory of MCP servers) on 16 November 2026 it appeared as an MCP server under the MIT licence, with about 2,100 npm downloads and an update two days earlier. The note itself flags that Glama's download column is unlabelled, so treat the figure as approximate and check npm and GitHub.
  • It is small and active.

What Grid does

  • Open source under Apache-2.0; not published to npm yet.
  • One plan file (walls, rooms, openings) as the source of truth, written rooms first, with 33 tools to read, measure, draw, check and change it.
  • Checks on the drawing, and figures from building codes (today the United States' model codes), each with its source and a status.

General guidance, not your local rules

That is the label on every Grid report: the checks are general guidance, and no Grid message says a plan complies or is approved.

Where they may differ

Both are tool servers for floor plans that an AI agent can call. This note does not hold enough about ArchLang's tools, formats or checks to compare them point by point, so none is claimed. Read both projects' pages and try them on your own plan.
